## 0. Changes from draft 1

1. **The contradiction needs only rational invertibility of $B$.** With the vanishing theorem in families in the form used here (Hypothesis (F) of §5), Theorem 1(i) suffices. It is the elementary Cayley–Hamilton statement, and it needs only $\det u_L\neq0$. The 2-adic statements of Theorem 1(ii) are not used. So the odd-determinant condition (B2′) is exactly what Theorem 1(ii) needs, but it is not "exactly what is needed" for the argument; Proposition 3.1 gives the corrected statement.
2. **The zero bound in the 2-adic theorem is sharpened, and the draft's sharpness claim is false.** The Taylor coefficients satisfy $v_2(c_k)\ge k+s_2(k)$, not merely $\ge k+1$, so a nonzero pairing $q$ is killed at most $\zeta(v_2(q))$ times along $e'\mathbb Z$, where $\zeta(v)=\max\{k\ge0:k+s_2(k)\le v\}$. Draft 1 claimed that the bound $v_2(q)-1$ "is attained". It cannot be attained whenever $v_2(q)-1$ is neither $0$ nor a power of $2$ (for example $v_2(q)=4,6,7,8$); with $v_2(q)=4$ at most two zeros occur, not three. The bound $\zeta$ is attained for every $v_2(q)\le9$ (§7.2).
3. **§4 of draft 1 used the identity "$2^{n_D-1}\Omega=\sum(\text{abelian ends})$".** It is the counting formula already flagged as false in `exposition.tex`. Only the vanishing form is used now: if the cut-down monopole one-manifold has no ends besides the instanton links and the cancelling lens ends, then $2^{n_D-1}\Omega=0$.
4. **New grading constraint (Proposition 5.4).** A negative-definite cobordism $V\colon Y_{-2}\to Y_2$ inducing a rational isomorphism forces $I(Y_2)$ to have equal rank in all eight degrees. Hence $\Delta_K''(1)=0$ and $\operatorname{rank}I(Y_2;\mathbb Q)\equiv0\pmod 8$, granted the exact triangles. So the one-sided statement holds outright, granted (T), for every knot with $\Delta''_K(1)\neq0$.

## 1. Setting

### 1.1 Floer groups and pairings

Let $Y$ be a closed oriented 3-manifold and $P\to Y$ an $SO(3)$-bundle of one of two kinds:
- **(a)** admissible: $w_2(P)$ pairs nontrivially with an integral class;
- **(b)** $Y$ is a rational homology sphere, $P$ is trivial, and every reducible flat connection is central.

Integral homology spheres are of kind (b), and so are $Y_{\pm2}=S^3_{\pm2}(K)$. Indeed $H_1(Y_{\pm2})=\mathbb Z/2$, so every abelian representation $\pi_1(Y_{\pm2})\to SU(2)$ takes values in $\{\pm1\}$. **[VERIFIED]**

Floer's complex $CF(Y;\mathbb Z)$ is generated by the irreducible critical points of a generically perturbed Chern–Simons functional, with the determinant-one gauge group and coherent orientations. It is a finitely generated free complex. It is $\mathbb Z/8$-graded in kind (b), with the absolute grading fixed relative to $\theta$, and relatively $\mathbb Z/4$-graded in the admissible case $(Y_0,w)$ (DLME Prop. 3.1(b)). Put
$$I(Y;R)=H_*(CF(Y)\otimes R),\qquad L(Y)=I(Y;\mathbb Z)/\mathrm{Tors},\qquad r(Y)=\operatorname{rank}L(Y).$$
For kind (b) the only point beyond the homology-sphere case is that a central flat connection has a three-dimensional stabilizer, so breaking there costs three dimensions (manuscript §2, Lemma "Reducibles at the ordinary ends"). **[PLAUSIBLE]**

Since $CF(-Y)=\operatorname{Hom}(CF(Y),\mathbb Z)$ up to a shift of grading, the Kronecker pairing $I(-Y;\mathbb Z)\otimes I(Y;\mathbb Z)\to\mathbb Z$ is defined. It kills torsion, so $\Psi_+\in I(-Y;\mathbb Z)$ determines $\psi_+\in\operatorname{Hom}(L(Y),\mathbb Z)$. **[VERIFIED: universal coefficients]**

**Lemma 1.1 (the twist acts freely).** Let $Y=S^3_{\pm2}(K)$ and let $\chi$ be the flat $\mathbb Z/2$-bundle with holonomy $-1$ on the meridian.
1. Tensoring with $\chi$ shifts the Chern–Simons functional by $\tfrac12$ mod $1$.
2. Hence $\chi$ fixes no flat $SU(2)$-connection, and no critical point of a $\chi$-invariant perturbation, up to determinant-one gauge.
3. $CF(Y;\mathbb F_2)$ is therefore a free $\mathbb F_2[\iota]/(\iota^2-1)$-module, where $\iota$ is the induced involution.

*Proof.*
- **The shift.** $A\mapsto\mathrm{CS}(\chi\otimes A)-\mathrm{CS}(A)$ is locally constant, because $\chi\otimes$ preserves $\operatorname{tr}(F_A\wedge a)$. The space of connections is connected, so the shift is a constant.
- **Its value.** At $\theta$ the shift is $\mathrm{CS}(\chi\theta)=\pm\operatorname{lk}(\mu,\mu)=\tfrac12$ mod $1$, since the linking form of $S^3_{\pm2}(K)$ is $\pm\tfrac12$ on $\mu$.
- **No fixed points.** If $\chi\alpha$ were gauge equivalent to $\alpha$, the shift would vanish.
- **Independent check for irreducibles.** A fixed irreducible $\rho$ would be binary dihedral with dihedral character $\chi$, so $\rho(\mu)^2=-1$. But $\mu^{\pm2}\lambda=1$, $\lambda\in\pi''$, and the image of $\rho$ is metabelian, so $\rho(\mu)^{2}=1$. This is a contradiction. ∎ **[VERIFIED]**

The grading shift of $\iota$ is $4$: the bundle $2F_l^*$ on $-C_2$ has $-2c^2=4$ (T5 §2.2). **[VERIFIED as arithmetic]**

### 1.2 Maps and their degrees

Integral chain maps arise from cobordisms $(W,c)$ with integral cut-down classes, and from families of metrics whose boundary faces contribute zero after signed sums. An example of the second kind is the map $B$ of §3, granted (B1). DLME's $g_1$ is of the same kind, but they construct it over $\mathbb F_2$ only (DLME Prop. 3.10).

For a cobordism between rational homology spheres with $b_1(W)=0$, a determinant class $c$ vanishing on the ends, a family of dimension $k$ and an insertion of degree $2d$, index additivity gives
$$\deg=-2c^2-3b^+(W)+k-2d\pmod 8.$$
This is the formula in the proof of DLME Prop. 3.4, with the same $\theta$-correction at rational homology sphere ends. Since $c|_{\partial W}=0$, $c$ lifts to $H_2(W)$ and $c^2\in\mathbb Z$. Hence:
- $\deg V_*=0$ for $V$ with $b_1=b^+=0$ and $c=0$;
- $\deg B=-2c^2-1\equiv-1$, for $c^2\in\{0,-4\}$;
- $\deg H=-2c^2-3$ is odd. The lattice $H_2(W_H)$ is even, so $\deg H\in\{-3,1\}\pmod 8$, depending on the determinant lift (§7.2).

## 2. Persistence of Floer pairings under iteration

**Theorem 1.** Let $(Y,P)$ be as in §1.1, $L=L(Y)$, $r=\operatorname{rank}L$. Let $u$ be an endomorphism of $I(Y;\mathbb Z)$ induced by an integral chain map, for instance by a cobordism $Y\to Y$ with integral cut-down classes and possibly a family of metrics. Let $u_L\in\operatorname{End}L$ be the induced map. Let $\Psi_-\in I(Y;\mathbb Z)$ and $\Psi_+\in I(-Y;\mathbb Z)$, and put
$$q=\langle\Psi_+,\Psi_-\rangle\neq0,\qquad a_M=\langle\Psi_+,u^M\Psi_-\rangle\quad(M\ge0).$$

**(i) Characteristic zero.** If $\det u_L\neq0$, then for all $k\ge1$ and $m_0\ge0$ some $m\in[m_0,m_0+r)$ has $a_{km}\ne0$. In particular $a_M\neq0$ for infinitely many $M$ in every progression $k\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$.

**(ii) Two-adic.** Suppose $\det u_L$ is odd. This holds whenever $u\otimes\mathbb F_2$ is an automorphism of $I(Y;\mathbb F_2)$. Let $e$ and $e'$ be the orders of $u_L$ in $GL(L/2L)$ and $GL(L/4L)$; then $e\mid e'\mid 2e$.
- **(a)** $a_M\equiv q\pmod{2^{1+v_2(M/e)}}$ for every $M\in e\mathbb Z_{>0}$. In particular $a_M\equiv q\pmod{2^N}$ whenever the order of $u_L$ in $GL(L/2^NL)$ divides $M$, and that order divides $e\,2^{N-1}$.
- **(b)** $a_M\neq0$ and $v_2(a_M)=v_2(q)$ for every $M\in e\,2^{v_2(q)}\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$.
- **(c)** There is $F(T)=\sum_kc_kT^k\in\mathbb Q_2[[T]]$ with the following properties:
  - $c_0=q$, and $v_2(c_k)\ge k+s_2(k)$ for $k\ge1$, where $s_2$ is the binary digit sum;
  - $F$ converges on $\mathbb Z_2$, and $F(m)=a_{e'm}$ for $m\ge0$.

  Hence $a_{e'm}=0$ for at most $\zeta(v_2(q))$ integers $m\ge0$, where
  $$\zeta(v)=\max\{k\ge0:\ k+s_2(k)\le v\}.$$
  One has $\zeta(v)\le\max(0,v-1)$, with equality iff $v\le1$ or $v-1$ is a power of $2$.

**(iii) Sharpness.**
- (i) fails without $\det u_L\ne0$: take $u=0$.
- (ii)(a),(b) fail without odd determinant: for $L=\mathbb Z$ and $u=2$, $a_M=2^Mq$.
- (ii)(c) fails along $e\mathbb Z$ in place of $e'\mathbb Z$: for $u=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ and $\psi_\pm=(1,1)$, $a_M=1+(-1)^M$.
- The bound $\zeta(v)$ is attained for every $v\le9$ (§7.2). The draft's bound $v-1$ is not attained for $v=4,6,7,8$.

**[VERIFIED]** (proofs below; random tests in §7.2). That $\zeta$ is attained for all $v$ is **[SPECULATIVE]**.

Part (ii)(a) is the congruence one writes down first; it is immediate once $u_L^M\equiv1\pmod{2^N}$. Part (i) is what the cosmetic argument actually needs (§3). Parts (ii)(b),(c) show that the pairings are 2-adically continuous in the number of iterations and vanish at most $\zeta(v_2(q))$ times along $e'\mathbb Z$. This is Skolem's $p$-adic method (the proof of the Skolem–Mahler–Lech theorem) at the prime $2$, with an explicit bound. Floer theory supplies the integrality and the mod-two certificate of invertibility.

### Proofs

**Lemma 2.1 (localization at two).** Let $f\colon C\to C'$ be a chain map of finitely generated free complexes such that $f\otimes\mathbb F_2$ is a quasi-isomorphism. Then $f$ induces an isomorphism $L(C)\otimes\mathbb Z_{(2)}\to L(C')\otimes\mathbb Z_{(2)}$. Its determinant in integral bases is odd, and in particular nonzero.

*Proof.*
- The cone $Z$ of $f$ is finite and free, with $H(Z\otimes\mathbb F_2)=0$.
- Universal coefficients give $H(Z)\otimes\mathbb F_2=0=\operatorname{Tor}(H(Z),\mathbb F_2)$, so $H(Z)$ is finite of odd order and $H(Z)\otimes\mathbb Z_{(2)}=0$.
- An isomorphism of finitely generated $\mathbb Z_{(2)}$-modules preserves torsion, so it induces an isomorphism of the free quotients. ∎

This is the manuscript's Lemma "The finite-lattice argument" (§5). **[VERIFIED]**

**Lemma 2.2.** If $A\in\operatorname{End}(\mathbb Z^r)$ and $A\equiv1\pmod 2$, then $A^m\equiv1\pmod{2^{1+v_2(m)}}$.

*Proof.* If $A=1+2^jX$ with $j\ge1$, then $A^2=1+2^{j+1}(X+2^{j-1}X^2)$. Induct on $v_2(m)$; odd powers preserve the congruence. ∎ **[VERIFIED]**

**Lemma 2.3 (two-adic logarithm).** Let $A\in\operatorname{End}(L\otimes\mathbb Z_2)$ with $A=1+4X$. Then:
- $\Lambda=\log A=\sum_{j\ge1}(-1)^{j+1}(4X)^j/j$ converges and lies in $4\operatorname{End}$, because $v_2(4^j/j)=2j-v_2(j)\ge2$;
- $\exp(T\Lambda)$ converges for $T\in\mathbb Z_2$, because $v_2(\Lambda^k/k!)\ge2k-v_2(k!)=k+s_2(k)$ by Legendre;
- $\exp(m\Lambda)=A^m$ for $m\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, since the formal identity $\exp\log(1+x)=1+x$ holds for absolutely convergent series in the closed commutative algebra generated by $X$. ∎ **[VERIFIED]**

*Proof of Theorem 1.*

(i) Put $b_m=a_{km}=\psi_+(A^m\psi_-)$ with $A=u_L^k$, and let $\chi_A(T)=\sum_{i=0}^r\gamma_iT^i$. Then $\gamma_0=\pm\det A\neq0$, and Cayley–Hamilton gives $\sum_i\gamma_ib_{m+i}=0$. If $b$ vanishes at $r$ consecutive indices, the recurrence applied forwards and (dividing by $\gamma_0$) backwards gives $b_0=q=0$. ∎

(ii)(a),(b)
- Lemma 2.1 gives $u_L\in GL(L\otimes\mathbb Z_{(2)})$. Lemma 2.2 applied to $u_L^e$ gives (a) and the bound $e\,2^{N-1}$ on the order modulo $2^N$; it also gives $e'\mid2e$.
- For (b), if $e\,2^{v}\mid M$ with $v=v_2(q)$, then $a_M\equiv q\pmod{2^{v+1}}$, so $v_2(a_M)=v$. ∎

(ii)(c)
- Apply Lemma 2.3 to $A=u_L^{e'}$ and put $F(T)=\psi_+(\exp(T\Lambda)\psi_-)$. Then $c_k=\psi_+(\Lambda^k\psi_-)/k!$, so $v_2(c_k)\ge k+s_2(k)$, and $F(m)=a_{e'm}$.
- **Strassmann's theorem**† (Cassels, *Local Fields*, Ch. 4†): a nonzero $F$ with $c_k\to0$ has at most $N$ zeros in $\mathbb Z_2$, where $N$ is the largest index with $|c_N|=\max_k|c_k|$.
- Since $|c_N|\ge|c_0|=2^{-v}$, we get $N+s_2(N)\le v$, that is, $N\le\zeta(v)$.
- Finally $\zeta(v)\le v-1$ for $v\ge1$, because $s_2(k)\ge1$ for $k\ge1$. Equality holds iff $(v-1)+s_2(v-1)\le v$, that is, iff $s_2(v-1)\le1$. ∎

*Examples attaining $\zeta$.* In each, $N$ is the nilpotent Jordan block with $Ne_{i+1}=e_i$, $\psi_-$ is the last basis vector, and $\psi_+$ is read off from the Mahler coefficients of $a$.
- $v=3$: $u=1+2N$ on $\mathbb Z^3$, $\psi_+=(1,-7,24)$, $a_M=2(M-2)(M-6)$, so $a_{2m}=8(m-1)(m-3)$; two zeros along $e'\mathbb Z=2\mathbb Z$. This is draft 1's example.
- $v=4$: $u=1+4N$ on $\mathbb Z^3$, $\psi_+=(1,-4,16)$, $a_m=8(m-1)(m-2)$; two zeros, which is the maximum $\zeta(4)$.
- $v=5$: $u=1+4N$ on $\mathbb Z^5$, $\psi_+=(1,-10,60,-280,1120)$, $a_m=\tfrac{32}3(m-1)(m-3)(m-5)(m-7)$; four zeros.

### 7.4 Attempts at counterexamples

1. **A norm obstruction to (B2).** Modulo two, $B$ lies in the image of $1+\mathrm{Ad}_\iota$. That would forbid invertibility if $\iota$ fixed generators. It does not (Lemma 1.1: Chern–Simons shift $\tfrac12$, and no binary dihedral representation with dihedral character $\chi$, because $\lambda\in\pi''$). No obstruction.
2. **A grading obstruction to $V$.** This produced Proposition 5.4. It is a constraint, not a contradiction, and it is consistent with Boyer–Lines and DLME Cor. 1.4.
3. **One-sided $V$ for slice knots.** $S^3_{\pm2}(K)$ is rationally homology cobordant to $\pm\mathbb{RP}^3$, which gives a negative-definite $V$ with $b_2=0$. But $V_*$ factors through $I(\mathbb{RP}^3)=0$, while $I(Y_2)\cong I^w(Y_0)\ne0$ for nontrivial $K$ (granted (T); KSE §7.8). Not an isomorphism, so not a counterexample to Conjecture C.
4. **The Strassmann bound.** Draft 1's claim of sharpness fails for $v_2(q)=4$; this is now corrected.
5. **Genus-one cancellation.** Not found in examples (Remark 4.3).
6. **Density one.** As in draft 1, (N), (G) and Theorem 1 force the vanishing in families to fail when positive pieces are adjacent. This is consistent with the exclusion window $(\tfrac15,\tfrac37)$. No contradiction with (F) as stated.
