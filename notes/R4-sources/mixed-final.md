For a reducible component $v_j=c_1(L_1)-c_1(L_2)\equiv w\pmod2$, $c_1(\mathfrak s)=K_j=\Lambda+v_j$, and the level is $\ell_j=\kappa_j+\frac14v_j^2\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ (FL2b Lemma 3.32, (3.64); Memoir (2.3.14)). A *chain* is a maximal set of consecutive reducible components (between broken three-spheres). For a component, $p_j$, $s_j$, $z_j$ are its retained parameters, assigned sphere insertions and cap degree, $q_j=d_a(\hat\Gamma_j)+p_j-2s_j-z_j$ its cut-down anti-self-dual index and $i_j=q_j+2n_a(\hat\Gamma_j)$ its cut-down monopole index ([R] Lemma 1.3, lens ends filled by the reference reducibles). For a set $\Gamma$ of components I call
$$\delta(\Gamma)=\sum_{j\in\Gamma}(i_j+4),\qquad \varepsilon_I(\Gamma)=\sum_{j\in\Gamma}(q_j+4)$$
its two *index sums*; for a chain without a cap, with $L$ pieces of which $m$ are positive, $s$ assigned insertions and $\Delta=e_a-e_b$, $\delta=6\kappa_\Gamma-m+L-2s+\Delta$ ([R] §3.1, re-derived). Positive pieces $P\cong\mathbb{CP}^2\#5\overline{\mathbb{CP}}{}^2$, negative pieces $N$, bits $(e,f)$ of the two adjacent spheres, lattice conventions of [R] §1.1.

## 0. Findings

1. **Local structure** (§2). At a limit with a component in $\mathcal M^{*,0}$ the isotropy is $\{\pm1\}\times\prod_{\rm asd}\{\pm1\}\times\prod_{\rm abelian}\mathrm U(1)$ ($\mathrm{SU}(2)$ when $v=0$). The deformation complex of an abelian component splits under $\mathrm U(1)_j\times S^1$ into blocks of weights $(0,0)$, $(2,0)$, $(1,1)$, $(-1,1)$ with indices $p-(1+b^+)$, $4\kappa_0-(1+b^+)$, $\frac18((\Lambda\pm v)^2-\sigma)$; the two spinor indices sum to $\Theta-\kappa_0$. The isotropy acts locally freely on the gluing data $\prod\mathrm{SU}(2)$; per abelian component the gluing data form $S^2\times\mathrm{SU}(2)$. Keeping gluing data the stratum has virtual dimension $1-k$, forgetting them $1-4k$. **VERIFIED** (by hand; 63,410 configurations; numerical group action). A Kuranishi model with gluing parameters is **PLAUSIBLE** and is not used.
2. **What cannot be done** (§2.3). On a union of pieces with $b^+=0$ every class $v\equiv w$ carries an abelian instanton for every metric and every gauge-equivariant perturbation, and the indices of the nonzero-weight blocks cannot change; multivalued equivariant terms do not help, the stabilizer being connected; the circle of an abelian component is not pinned by the phase of a component in $\mathcal M^{*,0}$. So abelian components can be neither removed nor regularized. **VERIFIED.**
3. **The necessary projection** of [R] §3.4 and [M1] Prop. 3.1 (forget the abelian components, keep the others and make them regular together) is a correct deduction, but it asks coupled regularity at *every* anti-self-dual and Seiberg–Witten component of a mixed limit. That is far more than needed. **VERIFIED** as logic.
4. **The correct count** is [M2]'s: forget every component outside $\mathcal M^{*,0}$. With perturbations acting on one piece at a time, a limit lies in (the $\mathcal M^{*,0}$-components cut down by everything, modulo the diagonal circle) $\times$ (the rest), and the first factor has dimension $D_F=5-4f-\sum_{j\notin F}(i_j+4)-\lambda_F$. The forgotten components enter only through their indices $i_j$, which are numbers. **VERIFIED** (deduction; 20,000 random faces).
5. **Index sums of chains** (§3.3, §3.6). A chain of reducible components without a cap has $\delta\ge-2$ at every spacing $\ge2$; at spacing $\ge3$ (the composite has spacing $\ge4$ throughout) $\delta=-2$ only for a positive piece alone between two broken three-spheres, bits $(0,1)$, both bounding insertions met on it, $\kappa=\frac12$. **VERIFIED** (proof from [R] Lemma 4.4; exact minimization over abelian, Seiberg–Witten and mixed chains at spacings 2–5, agreeing with [R]'s table).
6. **Theorem A** (§3.4). Every limit with $k\ge1$ and a component in $\mathcal M^{*,0}$ is excluded by $D_F\le-1$, with no hypothesis at abelian or Seiberg–Witten components, unless it contains a component of *low index*: an anti-self-dual component with $i\le-3$ (or, containing a cap, $i\le-1$ when $k\ge2$, $i\le-3$ when $k=1$), or a Seiberg–Witten component containing a blown-up cap whose chain has $\delta\le1$. **VERIFIED** as a deduction (exhaustive over arrangements; thresholds sharp).
7. **Correction to [M2] Thm. 3.3 and [FF] §2.3 (row 4), after [SWF] item 12.** [M2] Prop. 3.2(d) ("$\delta\ge2$ for every chain containing a cap and a Seiberg–Witten component") holds only for short chains. A Seiberg–Witten component containing a blown-up cap and $L$ negative pieces can have $i$ as low as about $-\frac18\lambda^2$ or below ($-81,-171,-388$ for $\lambda=21,31,51$ in my independent minimization), with $q\gg0$. Next to a component in $\mathcal M^{*,0}$ the count then fails. **VERIFIED** (count); existence of such components **SPECULATIVE**.
8. **The residual hypothesis (A3′)** (§3.5). Low-index components lie in two end regions fixed before $M$. There one needs coupled regularity through the phase of a component in $\mathcal M^{*,0}$: two-valued terms at low-index anti-self-dual components (single-valued terms provably vanish there), single-valued terms at long Seiberg–Witten cap components (their circle is pinned by the phase). With it, the kept problem has dimension $\le-1$. **PLAUSIBLE**, 55–60%. It involves no abelian component.
9. **Abelian instantons need no analytic hypothesis** (§3.7). They enter only through $\delta\ge-2$ for interior chains, a topological inequality. At the caps an abelian component would be fatal (index $\approx-\frac12\lambda^2$, unregularizable); it is excluded by (B2), a generic choice of the cap metric. **VERIFIED** as logic; (B2) **PLAUSIBLE** (85%).
10. **The margin** (§3.6). The count has a margin of exactly one: two components in $\mathcal M^{*,0}$ containing the caps around a chain with $\delta=-2$. *New:* every $\delta=-2$ configuration is absent under two routine conditions — its Seiberg–Witten versions all have tangential index $d_s=-2$ at a corner with no parameter (30 of 30 classes; proof), and its abelian version needs $v\cdot H=0$ at the fixed corner period. With them $\delta\ge-1$ for every interior chain, attained only by the abelian chains $NP$, $PN$, which are regular in every nonzero weight; the margin becomes two except next to cap components of index $-1$. **VERIFIED** (arithmetic) / conditions **PLAUSIBLE** (75%).
11. **Decision between [M1] and [M2]** (§3.8). [M2]'s count is right and needs much less than [M1]'s (A3\*); [M2] is wrong about long Seiberg–Witten cap chains and about the existence of a $\delta=-2$ Seiberg–Witten chain at the margin; [M1]'s local analysis, its stabilizer and gluing lemmas, its observation that the margin configurations are regular in nonzero weights, and its analysis of transport across necks are right and are kept.
12. **Alternatives** (§4). No choice of $w$ or $\Lambda$ and no holonomy perturbation removes abelian instantons; none is needed. Euler-class and localization arguments need an obstructed gluing theorem and are unnecessary for abelian components. For the residual: blowing up the caps never improves the anti-self-dual and Seiberg–Witten cap margins together (exact). **VERIFIED** / **SPECULATIVE** as marked.
13. **Missed strata.** None at abelian components. The long Seiberg–Witten cap components (found by [SWF], confirmed here) are the one stratum the two attempts missed. Twelve attempted counterexamples are in §6.
14. **Verdict.** "The hardest open point" of [S] is misplaced: abelian instantons are excluded by counting, with no hypothesis. The open point is (A3′) in the end regions, at anti-self-dual components of low index and at long Seiberg–Witten components containing a blown-up cap; it is needed already at codimension-one faces, without abelian components. Confidence: 90% that abelian components need no analytic hypothesis (given the shared inputs); 55–60% for (A3′); about 60% that mixed limits are excluded by this route given the inputs shared with the rest of [S] §4; about 30% (25–40%) for $2^{n_a-1}\Omega=0$ for large $M$ by this route.

## 1. Bookkeeping

### 1.1 Index identities

**Lemma 1.1** ([R] Lemma 1.3; [FF] §1.1). For an ideal limit over a face with $k$ broken three-spheres, with the charges of trajectories, points of concentration and lens caps allocated to components,
$$\sum_{j=0}^k(q_j+4)=4,\qquad\sum_{j=0}^ki_j-2(n_a-1)=2-4k .$$
*Proof.* $\sum_jd_a(\hat\Gamma_j)=d_a-3k$ (each broken $S^3$ removes $h^0(\theta)=3$; unframed indices), $\sum_jn_a(\hat\Gamma_j)=n_a$ (the twisted Dirac operator of the round $S^3$ has spectrum $\pm(\frac32+k)$ and is invertible), $\sum p_j=n-k$, $\sum s_j=n$, $\sum z_j=\deg z_C$, $\deg z=d_a+n$. ∎ **VERIFIED** as algebra (20,000 random faces built from per-piece data, `mf5_identities.py`); additivity across $S^3$ **PLAUSIBLE** (Atiyah–Patodi–Singer, standard).

Each broken three-sphere costs four: three for the gluing parameter, which an ideal limit does not record, and one for the lost neck parameter. **VERIFIED.**

### 1.2 The $\mu_c$-sections at a limit

**Lemma 1.2** ([FF] Lemma 1.1; [SWF] §1.2). A gauge-invariant function $f$ on configurations of a piece with $f(\zeta x)=\zeta^2f(x)$ vanishes at anti-self-dual, Seiberg–Witten and abelian components. Hence at an ideal limit each $\mu_c$-section restricts to the sum of the contributions of the components in $\mathcal M^{*,0}$. *Proof.* At $(A,0)$ every scalar fixes the configuration. At $(A_{L_1}\oplus A_{L_2},\Psi)$ the determinant-one gauge transformation $\mathrm{diag}(\zeta,\zeta^{-1})$ fixes the connection and multiplies $\Psi$ by $\zeta$. The sections are sums over pieces of functions sampled after transport inside the piece, and the gauge groups of the pieces of a broken limit are independent. ∎ **VERIFIED.**

So no condition defining $\mathcal W$ cuts a reducible or zero-spinor component: this is why Feehan and Leness either exclude reducible strata by level (FL2b Thm. 3.33(a), hypothesis (3.68)) or compute their links (Memoir Hyp. 7.8.1). **VERIFIED** as logic (FL2b Thm. 3.33 and (3.68) read; numbers recomputed).

## 2. Local structure at a mixed limit (question (a))

Fix an ideal limit $\zeta=(c_0,\dots,c_k)$ over a face with $k$ broken three-spheres, with at least one component in $\mathcal M^{*,0}$ and at least one abelian component. No abelian component contains a cap piece (§3.7), so the abelian components are interior and $k\ge2$. **VERIFIED** given (B2).

### 2.1 The equations and their linearization

On each piece, in FL2a's form, the $\mathrm{SO}(3)$-monopole equations for $(A,\Phi)$ on $\mathfrak t=(\rho,V=W\otimes E)$ read $F^+_{\hat A}-\tau\rho^{-1}(\Phi\otimes\Phi^*)_{00}=0$, $D_A\Phi+\rho(\vartheta)\Phi=0$, plus perturbations, with the fixed determinant connection; on a family the metric is $g_t$, $t\in Q_j$ (the parameters internal to the component). The deformation complex at $(A,\Phi,t)$ is
$$0\to\Omega^0(\mathfrak{su}(E))\xrightarrow{\ d^0\ }\Omega^1(\mathfrak{su}(E))\oplus\Omega^0(V^+)\oplus T_tQ_j\xrightarrow{\ d^1\ }\Omega^+(\mathfrak{su}(E))\oplus\Omega^0(V^-)\to0,$$
$d^0\xi=(-d_A\xi,\xi\Phi)$, and $d^1$ the linearization in $(a,\phi,\dot t)$. At $\Phi=0$ it splits as the anti-self-dual complex of $\hat A$ plus the coupled Dirac operator (FL2a Lemma 3.4: $H^1_{A,0}\cong H^1_{\hat A}\oplus\ker(D_A+\rho(\vartheta))$, $H^2_{A,0}\cong H^2_{\hat A}\oplus\mathrm{coker}(D_A+\rho(\vartheta))$). **VERIFIED** (read; FL2a numbers recomputed, §10). With parameters, the derivative in $t$ of the curvature equation at a reducible lies in the diagonal, and that of the Dirac equation at $\Phi=0$ vanishes. **VERIFIED.**

### 2.2 The four blocks at an abelian component

**Proposition 2.1.** Let $c_b=(A_{L_1}\oplus A_{L_2},0)$ be abelian, $\mathfrak{su}(E)=i\underline{\mathbb R}\oplus L$, $L=L_1L_2^{-1}$, $c_1(L)=v$, $\kappa_0=-\frac14v^2$, $V^+=W^+\otimes L_1\oplus W^+\otimes L_2$. Under $\mathrm U(1)_b\times S^1$ ($\mathrm U(1)_b=\{\mathrm{diag}(z,z^{-1})\}$, $S^1$ the phase) the complex is the direct sum of:

| block | complex | weight | $H^0$ | $H^2$ | index |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (W0) | $\Omega^0(i\mathbb R)\to\Omega^1(i\mathbb R)\oplus T_tQ_b\to\Omega^+(i\mathbb R)$ | $(0,0)$ | $i\mathbb R$ | $H^+(\hat\Gamma_b)$ modulo the derivative of the period map | real $p_b-(1+b^+_b)$ |
| (W2) | $\Omega^0(L)\to\Omega^1(L)\to\Omega^+(L)$ | $(2,0)$ | $0$ | $\mathrm{coker}\,d^+_{A_L}$ on $\ker d^*_{A_L}$ | complex $4\kappa_0-(1+b^+_b)$ |
| (W+) | $D^+_{A_{L_1}}:W^+\otimes L_1\to W^-\otimes L_1$ | $(1,1)$ | — | $\mathrm{coker}\,D_{\Lambda+v}$ | complex $\frac18((\Lambda+v)^2-\sigma)$ |
| (W−) | $D^+_{A_{L_2}}:W^+\otimes L_2\to W^-\otimes L_2$ | $(-1,1)$ | — | $\mathrm{coker}\,D_{\Lambda-v}$ | complex $\frac18((\Lambda-v)^2-\sigma)$ |

The two spinor indices sum to $n_a(\hat\Gamma_b)=\Theta_b-\kappa_0$ and differ by $\frac12\Lambda\cdot v$; the total real index is $d_a(\hat\Gamma_b)+2n_a(\hat\Gamma_b)+p_b$. Insertions $V_{S_i}$ carried by $c_b$ with $\langle v,S_i\rangle\ne0$ are met identically (incidence (J2) of [R] Lemma 1.2), so they cut nothing although the index counts each as two real conditions. When $v=0$ (trivial connection, stabilizer $\mathrm{SU}(2)$), (W0) and (W2) merge into the trivial anti-self-dual complex and the two spinor blocks into one with the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ acting. **VERIFIED**: the anti-self-dual index $8\kappa_0-3(1+b^+)$ of an $\mathrm{SO}(3)$ reducible minus the abelian part $-(1+b^+)$ gives (W2); the spin$^c$ structures of the two lines have $c_1=\Lambda\pm v$; integrality, the sum and the total index checked on 63,410 configurations of single pieces and of unions $PN$ (`mf1_blocks.py`).

### 2.3 Isotropy and what equivariance forbids

**Proposition 2.2** ([M1] §2.3, [M2] Prop. 2.1). The stabilizer of the main components of $\zeta$ in $\prod_j\mathcal G_j\times S^1$ ($\mathcal G_j$ the determinant-one gauge group of $\hat\Gamma_j$, unframed at the ends) consists of the $((g_j),z)$ with $z=\pm1$; $g_j=z$ on components in $\mathcal M^{*,0}$ and on Seiberg–Witten components (whose stabilizer $\mathrm{diag}(\eta,\eta^{-1})$ must satisfy $z\eta=1$); $g_j\in\{\pm1\}$ arbitrary on anti-self-dual components; $g_j\in\mathrm U(1)_j$ (or $\mathrm{SU}(2)$) arbitrary on abelian components. So it is $\{\pm1\}\times\prod_{\rm asd}\{\pm1\}\times\prod_{\rm abelian}\mathrm U(1)$; the diagonal $-1$ acts trivially; the factor $-1$ of an anti-self-dual component acts by $-1$ on its Dirac cokernel and trivially on everything else. **VERIFIED.**

**Proposition 2.3** (persistence; [M2] Prop. 2.3, [M1] Lemma 2.1). (a) On a closed-up union of pieces with $b_1=b^+=0$, for every metric of the family and every bounded continuous gauge-equivariant perturbation, every class $v\equiv w$ carries a zero-spinor reducible solution, unique if the perturbation is small. (b) The linearization of every $\mathrm U(1)_b\times S^1$-equivariant perturbation, single- or multivalued, preserves the four blocks, so each block keeps its index; after generic equivariant perturbation the weight-$\pm1$ cokernels have complex dimension $\max(0,-\mathrm{ind}(W\pm))$ and no equivariant perturbation lowers it. (c) The Kuranishi map of the component is $\mathrm U(1)_b$-equivariant, so its value at the abelian point lies in weight zero. *Proof.* (a) At a reducible an equivariant perturbation of the curvature equation takes values in the fixed part of the stabilizer, $i\Lambda^+$; an equivariant perturbation of the Dirac equation vanishes at $\Phi=0$. So the equations reduce to $F^+_a=\eta(a)$ for the $\mathrm U(1)$ connection $a$ on $L$; $d^+:\ker d^*\to\Omega^+$ is an isomorphism, and Schauder's (or, for small $\eta$, Banach's) fixed point theorem applies. (b) Schur's lemma; a compact perturbation of a Fredholm block preserves its index; a finite list of branches permuted by a connected group consists of invariant branches. (c) $\chi(0)$ is fixed by the stabilizer. ∎ **VERIFIED.**

So, as [S] says, abelian components on chains with negative spinor indices "cannot be made regular": the phase of a component in $\mathcal M^{*,0}$ pins the common phase, not the independent circle $\mathrm U(1)_b$. **VERIFIED.** The point of §3 is that this does not matter.

### 2.4 The other components

- *Component in $\mathcal M^{*,0}$*: the full complex, $H^0=0$; stabilizer $\{\pm1\}$ acting trivially. **VERIFIED.**
- *Anti-self-dual component*: $H^\bullet=H^\bullet_{\hat A}\oplus(\ker,\mathrm{coker})(D_A+\rho(\vartheta))$ (FL2a Lemma 3.4, Cor. 3.6). Regular for its insertions, $H^2_{\hat A}=0$; the obstruction is the Dirac cokernel, of complex dimension $\ge-n_a(\hat\Gamma_j)$, on which the factor $-1$ acts by $-1$. **VERIFIED.**
- *Seiberg–Witten component*: tangential complex of the Seiberg–Witten equations for $K=\Lambda+v$ with $\eta=F^+_{A_\Lambda}$ (FL2a Lemma 3.12, read), index $d_s=\frac14(K^2-2\chi-3\sigma)$, plus the normal complex (the off-diagonal anti-self-dual complex on $L$ and the Dirac operator on $W^+\otimes L_2$), on which its circle $\{(\mathrm{diag}(z,z^{-1}),z^{-1})\}$ acts with weights $\pm2$; FL2a Thm. 3.29 computes the Chern character of this normal bundle. With a component in $\mathcal M^{*,0}$ that circle is pinned to $\pm1$. **VERIFIED** (read; numbers recomputed).

### 2.5 Gluing data

At a broken $J_\ell$ both sides converge to $(\theta,0)$, whose stabilizer is the constant $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ (its centre acting by $-1$ on spinors). The gluing parameter is $\rho_\ell\in\mathrm{SU}(2)$, an identification of the fibres of $E$ (with their determinant) at the two ends; the neck length is the cube coordinate near the face. The isotropy acts by $\rho_\ell\mapsto g_{\ell-1}\rho_\ell g_\ell^{-1}$ through the limiting values of the stabilizing gauge transformations; $S^1$ acts trivially.

**Proposition 2.4** ([M1] Lemma 2.2, [M2] Prop. 2.4). (a) If the two outer components of a string of abelian components are not abelian, every element of the isotropy fixing the gluing data is central: the stabilizer of the gluing data is finite. (b) For one abelian component $c_b$ between $J_b$ and $J_{b+1}$, $\mathrm U(1)_b$ acts freely on $\mathrm{SU}(2)_b\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{b+1}$ and $(\rho_b,\rho_{b+1})\mapsto(\rho_b\mathrm U(1)_b,\rho_b\rho_{b+1})$ identifies the quotient with $S^2\times\mathrm{SU}(2)$: the $S^2$ records the line of the reduction seen from the left neighbour, the $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ the gluing parameter of the two neighbours across $c_b$. (c) The factor $-1$ of an anti-self-dual component acts by $\rho\mapsto-\rho$ on its two gluing parameters. Hence $(\prod_\ell\mathrm{SU}(2))/(\text{isotropy})$ is an orbifold of dimension $3k-\#\{\text{abelian, }v\ne0\}-3\#\{\text{abelian, }v=0\}$. *Proof.* (a) The neighbours' stabilizer values are central, $g_{a-1}=c$; $\rho_a=c\rho_ag_a^{-1}$ forces $g_a=c$, and inductively. (b), (c) direct. ∎ **VERIFIED** (proof; numerically, the infinitesimal action of the abelian torus on the gluing data has smallest singular value $\ge1.04$ on random data for eight patterns of components, and the invariants of (b) are exact to $4\cdot10^{-16}$: `mf7_gluing.py`).

So the isotropy of the *limit* is positive-dimensional, but that of the *gluing data* is finite: the abelian obstruction is a phenomenon of infinite neck length. **VERIFIED.**

*The obstruction space of the limit and its isotropy representation.* $H^2(\zeta)=\bigoplus_jH^2(c_j)$: zero (or $H^2$ of the cut-down problem) at components in $\mathcal M^{*,0}$; $\mathrm{coker}(D_A+\rho(\vartheta))$ at anti-self-dual components, where the factor $-1$ of that component acts by $-1$ and the phase by scalars; the normal cokernel at Seiberg–Witten components, of weight $\pm2$ under their circle, which the phase pins; and at abelian components the weight-zero part $H^+(\hat\Gamma_b)$ modulo the period derivative together with $\mathrm{coker}\,d^+_{A_L}$ (weight $2$) and the two spinor cokernels (weights $\pm1$), on which $\mathrm U(1)_b$ acts independently of the phase. In Donaldson's description an isolated $\mathrm U(1)$-reducible has the neighbourhood $\chi^{-1}(0)/\mathrm U(1)$, a cone on a weighted projective space when it is unobstructed in nonzero weights. At a mixed limit $\mathrm U(1)_b$ also acts, freely, on the gluing data, so the quotient is a bundle over $S^2\times\mathrm{SU}(2)$ (Prop. 2.4(b)) rather than a cone: the cone point is resolved by the gluing parameter, and the nonzero-weight cokernels become an honest obstruction bundle over it. **VERIFIED** (group theory) / the bundle description **PLAUSIBLE** (it presupposes the model of Prop. 2.5).

### 2.6 The Kuranishi model (not used)

**Proposition 2.5** (PLAUSIBLE; it needs an obstructed gluing theorem in families with faces, the broken-neck analogue of Memoir Hyp. 7.8.1 and of Memoir Lemma 11.3.1 with Hyp. 11.3.5). A neighbourhood of $\zeta$ in the compactified family is $\mathfrak o^{-1}(0)/(\text{isotropy})$, with

## 4. Alternatives (question (c))

1. **A choice of $w$ or $\Lambda$ on the negative pieces.** Impossible. At zero spinor $\Lambda$ does not enter; on a $b^+=0$ union every $v\equiv w$ carries an abelian instanton (Prop. 2.3); $w_0$ must be even on the halves, and as $e$ varies both parities $e_j+e_{j+1}$ occur on every negative piece (trivial connection for even parity, $\kappa_0\ge\frac12$ for odd). $\Lambda$ only splits $\Theta-\kappa_0$ between the weights $\pm1$, and $\langle\Lambda_0,S_i\rangle=2$ fixes $\Theta$. **VERIFIED.** It is also unnecessary.
2. **Holonomy perturbations.** Equivariant ones preserve the reducible locus and the block indices (Prop. 2.3). Perturbations transporting the spinor across a broken neck break the abelian circle (it acts on the gluing parameter, Prop. 2.4) but make the limits framed: at a face with one broken neck the limiting problem gains three dimensions and acquires ends of virtual dimension zero that the relation cannot absorb ([M1] §4.2; [FF] Prop. 2.1(c)). **VERIFIED** as a count; that no arrangement of cut-offs repairs this, **PLAUSIBLE**.
3. **Obstruction bundle and Euler class** (Donaldson's cones over $\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}$ at reducibles, Taubes's obstruction sections, Kotschick–Morgan and Memoir Lemma 11.4.2, $e=(-\nu)^r$). Unnecessary: the stratum is empty by Theorem A. 4. **A dimension count that keeps the abelian instantons.** That is Proposition 3.1: they stay in the index identity through their $i_j$, bounded below by incidence and energy, and nothing else is asked of them. Counting them by their *actual* dimension in the fibre product ([M1] Prop. 3.1) gives a weaker count; counting them by their virtual dimension alone, as a stratum of its own, would be wrong (actual exceeds virtual). The equivariant reading: a mixed stratum is (a free $S^1$-space carrying all of $\mu_c$) $\times$ (an $S^1$-fixed space), so it meets $\mathcal Z_e$ only if the first factor's cut-down is nonempty. **VERIFIED.**
5. **A different family.** The three-sphere faces are forced ($B$ is the difference of two null-homotopies), and mixed limits need two broken three-spheres, i.e. corners, which every product family has. A generic corner template removes the extremal abelian chain (Prop. 3.4). More copies of $\phi_*B$ do not change the worst chain (the bound at $m=1$ is independent of the spacing). **VERIFIED** / (ii) **PLAUSIBLE**.
6. **Localization (Pidstrigach–Tyurin, Okonek–Teleman, Teleman).** Abelian and anti-self-dual components are fixed by the circle, a mixed limit is not; a fixed-point formula would need a virtual class on the compactified family space including its corners, i.e. the same gluing. The only localization content used is the product structure of Prop. 3.1. **PLAUSIBLE.**
7. **Removing the residual by redesigning the caps.** One blow-up with $\langle\Lambda,e\rangle=\lambda$ changes the anti-self-dual cap margin $a=\Theta_{\rm cap}-\kappa_{\min}$ and the Seiberg–Witten cap margin $b=\min_K(-\frac14(K-\Lambda)^2)-\kappa_{\min}$ by $\Delta a+\Delta b=\frac12(1-|\lambda|)$ ($\lambda$ odd) or $-\frac12|\lambda|$ ($\lambda$ even, one extra insertion): never positive, zero only for $|\lambda|\le1$ (`mf6_tradeoff.py`). So large $\lambda_\pm$ trades one residual for the other, and blow-ups cannot remove both. **VERIFIED.** Moving $\Lambda|_{\rm cap}$ the other way (towards the positive cone, so that anti-self-dual cap components have $i\ge0$) lowers $b$ by as much as it raises $a$; once $b<0$, Seiberg–Witten strata of the caps reach nonnegative level, and on the unbroken manifold they would meet the closure of $\mathcal Z_e$, which only the gluing of Memoir Hyp. 7.8.1 could handle. So that route leaves the vanishing argument. **PLAUSIBLE.** Whether caps with $b^-\gtrsim\frac92b^++\frac12\deg z_C$ ([M2] §5, item 7) give $a,b\ge0$ together is **SPECULATIVE**.
8. **Shorter negative stretches.** If the nonvanishing could be arranged without five consecutive copies of $\phi_*B$ in $\Pi_0$, the interior low-index anti-self-dual components would disappear; the cap residuals would remain. **SPECULATIVE.**
