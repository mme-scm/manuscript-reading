**Notation.**
- $Y_r=S^3_r(K)$, $X_r=X_r(K)$ is the trace, and $W'=(-C_2)\cup_{S^3}C_{-2}$ carries $B$. The sphere is $S=F_l-F_r$, with $S^2=-4$.

- Unless stated otherwise, Floer groups are Floer's irreducible groups. They use the determinant-one gauge group, the trivial $SO(3)$-bundle on $Y_{\pm2}$, and the cobordism maps of that bundle.

**Definition.** A cobordism $W\colon Y\to Y'$, with $Y,Y'\in\{Y_2,Y_{-2}\}$, is a *negative-definite mod-2 equivalence* if:
- $b_1(W)=b^+(W)=0$, and
- $W_*\colon I(Y;\mathbb F_2)\to I(Y';\mathbb F_2)$ is an isomorphism.

Since $\dim I(Y_2;\mathbb F_2)=\dim I(Y_{-2};\mathbb F_2)$ (via $B$), "injective" suffices. The map $W_*$ is integral, so by the finite-lattice lemma (`stack:finite-lattice`) it is an isomorphism over $\mathbb Z_{(2)}$ and over $\mathbb Q$.

## 0. Conclusions

## 2. Statements

### 2.1 Theorems

**Theorem A (two-sided).** Let $K$ be a nontrivial knot. There are no negative-definite mod-2 equivalences
$$V\colon S^3_{-2}(K)\to S^3_2(K),\qquad V'\colon S^3_2(K)\to S^3_{-2}(K)$$
such that $\pi_1(V')$ is normally generated by the image of $\pi_1$ of one of its two ends.

*Status.* The theorem is **[S]**, because it inherits §§6–10 (closed family $\mathrm{PU}(2)$ vanishing). Given those sections, the passage from $\varphi$ to $(V,V')$ is:
- **[V]** for the topology, the dimension count, energy and Dirac index (§3);
- **[P]** for transversality on $V$-pieces. That transversality comes from generic holonomy perturbations on $V$; no geometry is placed on $V$.

*Proof outline.* Run §§4–10 with $V'$ in place of $\varphi^{-1}$ in the caps and $V$ in place of $\varphi$ in the composite cobordism.
1. **Caps.** $T'=f_-V'f_+$ is a $\mathbb Q$-quasi-isomorphism. Labelled surfaces stay in the $f_\pm$ halves, $X'_k$ is simply connected, and Cayley–Hamilton gives some $k\ge1$ with a nonzero pairing.
2. **Repetition.** $\mathcal A=V_*B$ and $\mathcal D=HB$ are isomorphisms mod 2 of $L=I(Y_2;\mathbb Z)/\mathrm{Tor}$, so $(\mathcal A^3\mathcal D)^M\equiv1\pmod{2^N}$ and $\Omega\equiv\pm q\ne0$.
3. **Indices.** By Prop. 3.2, $n_D=\frac{5m-n}8+\mathrm{const}$ is unchanged.
4. **Reducible exclusion.** By Props. 3.3–3.4, every lower bound on charges and Feehan–Leness levels holds verbatim. In the round-3 form: $-v^2/4\ge(n-m)/2-C$ and $8\kappa=n+3m+O(1)$, so no Seiberg–Witten class contributes once $3n>7m$.
5. **Gluing.** By Prop. 3.5, the gluing identification of $\Omega$ with the composition survives.
6. **Contradiction.** $2^{n_D-1}\Omega=0$ with $n_D\ge1$ contradicts $\Omega\ne0$. ∎

**Theorem B (Chern–Simons part).**

*Assumptions.*
- DLME's $IP$-structure on $I(Y_{\pm2};\mathbb F_2)$. DLME assert this exists in the closing remark of their introduction.
- Their Prop. 3.3 for cobordisms between $Y_{\pm2}$ with $b_1=b^+=0$. This is [P]: central reducibles are the only new feature.

Let $K$ be nontrivial, so $\ell(Y_{\pm2})$ is finite, since $I(Y_2;\mathbb F_2)\cong I^w(Y_0;\mathbb F_2)\ne0$ by Kronheimer–Mrowka† and DLME Cor. `nonvanishing`. Then:
- (i) No pair $V,V'$ of negative-definite mod-2 equivalences has $\eta(V,0)+\eta(V',0)>0$. In particular neither $V$ nor $V'$ can be simply connected. No $\pi_1$ hypothesis is needed.
- (ii) Assume the ordinary-layer facts that $B$ is integral, an isomorphism mod 2, and of degree $-1$ and level $-\eta_B<0$ (the lift $c=\mathrm{PD}(S)$ has $(D,L)=(7,1-\eta)$, equivalent under periodicity). Then there is no single negative-definite mod-2 equivalence $V\colon Y_{-2}\to Y_2$ with $\eta(V)\ge\frac18$.

*Proof.*
- DLME Prop. 3.3 and Lemma `kappa-ineq`(b) give $\ell(Y_2)\le\ell(Y_{-2})-\eta_V$ and $\ell(Y_{-2})\le\ell(Y_2)-\eta_{V'}$. This proves (i).
- For (ii), $B$ gives $\ell(Y_{-2})\le\ell(Y_2)+\frac18-\eta_B$. Hence $\eta_V+\eta_B\le\frac18$. ∎

*Status.* **[P]**. It is independent of §§6–10.

*Corollary.* A cosmetic $\varphi$ (with $\eta_\varphi=0$) forces $\eta_B\le\frac18$. A lower bound $\eta(W',c)>\frac18$ for both lifts would therefore settle the $\pm2$ case by DLME's formalism alone. **[V as logic]**. I see no reason to expect such a bound **[S]**.

**Conjecture C (one-sided).** For nontrivial $K$ there is no negative-definite mod-2 equivalence $S^3_{-2}(K)\to S^3_2(K)$. **[S]**

This is the exact $\pm2$ analogue of Prop. 4.2. A spin-filtered Floer theory (B1/B3) would prove it directly, since that theory needs only $I(Y_2)\ne0$, not caps.

### 2.2 Why the closed form is two-sided, and what one-sidedness needs

- **The caps need a return without families.** The caps need a cobordism from $Y_2$ back to $Y_{-2}$ (or to $Y_0$) that is an isomorphism mod 2 and carries no family of metrics. Then:
  - the labelled probes (classes $h$ with $h\cdot A=1$, which must cross every copy of $Y_0$) can be moved into fixed caps by Mayer–Vietoris at a rational seam (§4, "Moving all probes");
  - the caps are fixed manifolds to which the period and isolation estimates (`estimates:cap`) apply.
- **No such return exists among the available maps.** Maps from surgery triangles increase the slope ($\infty\to n\to n+1\to\infty$). A return from $Y_2$ to lower slope either passes through $Y_\infty=S^3$, where the irreducible group vanishes, or uses a null-homotopy family ($g_\pm$ or $B$). The statement about the maps is **[V]**; that no other return exists is **[P]**.
- **What happens without $\varphi^{-1}$.** The caps would contain $T_B^k=f_-B(HB)^{k-1}f_+$, i.e. positive bridges at spacing one. These are exactly the configurations for which the $\mathrm{PU}(2)$ vanishing *must* fail (B1 Cor. 3.9).
- **The spacing is invisible to rational pairings [V].** Let $a=V_*B$ and $d=HB$.
  - Since $a$ is invertible over $\mathbb Q$, Cayley–Hamilton gives $\operatorname{span}\{a^k:k\ge3\}=\mathbb Q[a]\ni1$.
  - So the span of the admissible words (at least three $a$'s between consecutive $d$'s) is the whole algebra generated by $a$ and $d$; in particular it contains every $d^j$.
  - Consequently, if fixed caps $C_-=X_0^-\cup f_+$ and $C_+=f_-\cup X_0^+$, with probes in their interiors, satisfy
    $$\text{(N)}\qquad \langle y_+,\,B\,(HB)^j\,x_-\rangle\ne0\ \text{ for some }j\ge0,$$
    then some admissible word $w$ has $\langle y_+,Bwx_-\rangle\ne0$.
  - The proof of Theorem A then gives Conjecture C.
- **Why (N) does not follow from §4.** The labelled Cayley–Hamilton argument of §4 places probes on the intermediate copies of $W_H$. Each such copy contains a $Y_0$ that $h$ must cross.
- **Why probes on $A$ and points do not suffice.** With probes only on $A$ and points, the $e^{(2g-2)t}$ coefficient is $\sum_t(-1)^t\beta_{K_\omega+2t\,\mathrm{PD}(A)}$, since $w\cdot A$ is odd. This sum can cancel. That cancellation is why §4 uses classes with $h\cdot A=1$. **[P†]**

## 3. Replacing $\varphi$ by $V$: dimension count, energy, Dirac index

**Lemma 3.1 (negative cell).** Let $V$ satisfy $b_1=b^+=0$, and put $r=b_2(V)+2$. Then:
- (a) $N_V$ is closed with $b_1=b^+=0$, and $H_2(N_V;\mathbb Z)/\mathrm{Tor}\cong -I_r$.
- (b) Up to automorphism, $(F_r,F'_l)$ is one of:
  - $(e_1+e_2,\ \pm(e_1-e_2))$, the "shared" case; this is the only case when $r\le3$, in particular for $N_\varphi$;
  - $(e_1+e_2,\ \pm e_3\pm e_4)$, the "disjoint" case.
- (c) There is a characteristic $\Lambda_0\in\{\pm1\}^r$ with $\Lambda_0\cdot F_r=-2$, $\Lambda_0\cdot F'_l=0$ and $\Lambda_0^2=-r=\sigma(N_V)$. Hence $\Theta(N_V,\Lambda_0)=0$, and $\Theta(N_V,\Lambda_e)=(e_j-e_{j+1})/2$ for $\Lambda_e=\Lambda_0-e_jF_r+e_{j+1}F'_l$. This is exactly `stack:cell-theta` with $m_{\rm cell}=0$.
- (d) Every characteristic $K$ satisfies $K^2\le\sigma(N_V)$. Hence the Seiberg–Witten dimension $\tfrac14\big(K^2-(2\chi+3\sigma)\big)\le-\tfrac12(\chi+\sigma)=-1$.
- (e) **Relative form.** Under the rational orthogonal splitting $H_2(N_V;\mathbb Q)=\langle F_r\rangle\oplus H_2(V;\mathbb Q)\oplus\langle F'_l\rangle$,
  $$\Lambda_0^2=\tfrac{(\Lambda_0F_r)^2}{F_r^2}+\Lambda_V^2+\tfrac{(\Lambda_0F'_l)^2}{F'^2_l}=-2+\Lambda_V^2 .$$
  So $\Lambda_V^2=\sigma(V)$, i.e. $\Theta_V:=\tfrac14(\Lambda_V^2-\sigma(V))=0$. The trace halves contribute $-\tfrac14$ and $+\tfrac14$, as in B4 §2.

*Proof.*
- (a) Mayer–Vietoris over $\mathbb Q$ along the rational homology spheres $Y_{\pm2}$ gives $b_1=0$ and additivity of the forms, hence $b^+=0$. Donaldson's theorem (any $\pi_1$; Donaldson 1987†) gives the diagonal form.
- (b) The vectors of square $-2$ are $\pm e_i\pm e_j$. Two of them are orthogonal iff their supports are equal (with opposite relative sign) or disjoint; supports sharing one index pair to $\pm1$.
- (c) Choose the coordinates of $\Lambda_0$ on the supports of $F_r,F'_l$ accordingly. The other coordinates are arbitrary $\pm1$. The telescope is
  $$\Lambda_e^2-\Lambda_0^2=4e_j-2e_j-2e_{j+1}=2(e_j-e_{j+1}).$$
- (d) Each coordinate of $K$ is odd. Also $\chi+\sigma=2$.
- (e) Rational orthogonal splitting along rational homology spheres.

`checks.py` §3 verifies (b)–(d) for $r\le5$. ∎ **[V]**

**Prop. 3.2 (topology of the capped composite).** Let $X$, respectively $X^V$, be the 4-manifold obtained by capping the composite cobordism with $\varphi$, respectively with copies $V_i$ in its place, using the lifts of `stack:lifts`, extended by $c_0|_{V_i}=0$ and by Lemma 3.1(c). Then:
- (a) $b_1(X^V)=0$ and $b^+(X^V)=b^+(X)=m+b_0$;
- (b) $\sigma$ and $\Lambda_e^2$ both drop by $\sum_ib_2(V_i)$, while $c_e^2=c_0^2-4\sum e_i$ is unchanged;
- (c) $\Theta(X^V)=\Theta(X)=m+\Theta_0$ for every bit vector $e$;
- (d) the dimension count $8\kappa-3(1+b^+)=n+z$ gives the same $\kappa$, and
  $$n_D=\Theta-\kappa=\frac{5m-n}8+\Theta_0-\frac{z+3+3b_0}8,$$
  as in `stack:dirac-growth`; the $\mathrm{PU}(2)$ moduli dimension $D_I+2n_D$ and the number $n_D-1$ of phase cuts are unchanged;
- (e) the congruences for integral $c_2$ are unchanged.

*Proof.* $X^V$ is the connected sum, along the $S^3$'s $J_i$, of its cells, so $H_2$ splits orthogonally over $\mathbb Z$. Each $N_\varphi$ is replaced by $N_{V_i}$, with $c_0=0$ there. Then:
- (b) and (c) follow from Lemma 3.1(c),(e);
- (a) follows because $V_i$ adds no $b^+$ and no $b_1$;
- (d) holds because the ASD index $8\kappa-3(1-b_1+b^+)$ involves neither $\sigma$ nor $\Lambda$, while the Dirac index $\tfrac14(\Lambda^2-\sigma)-\kappa$ sees only their difference.

The closed index formula $\operatorname{ind}D_A=\int\operatorname{ch}(E)e^{l/2}\hat A=\tfrac14(\Lambda^2-\sigma)-\kappa$ holds for any closed 4-manifold, so no simple connectivity is involved. ∎ **[V]**

The client's phrasing mixes two separate facts: the ASD dimension count is unchanged because $b^+$, $b_1$ and $c_0$ are unchanged, while the *Dirac* index is unchanged because $\Theta_V=0$.

**Prop. 3.3 (energy lower bounds).** Let $v\in H^2(X^V;\mathbb Z)$ with $v\equiv c_e\pmod2$. On each $N_V$:
- (i) $v\cdot F_r$ and $v\cdot F'_l$ are even;
- (ii) $v^2|_{N_V}\le-\tfrac12\big((vF_r)^2+(vF'_l)^2\big)$, with equality iff $v|_{N_V}\in\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb Q}(F_r,F'_l)$ modulo torsion;
- (iii) $v^2|_{N_V}\equiv0$ or $2\pmod4$, the same two residues as on $N_\varphi$.

Consequently every lower bound of §5 and §8 holds verbatim on $X^V$:
- the bounds on $8\kappa_i=-2v_i^2+8\ell_i$;
- "a nonzero even half evaluation costs 2 in $-v^2$";
- $\kappa_{\rm piece}\in\tfrac12\mathbb Z$;
- the positive-square lemma with the neighbouring halves;
- the end-exclusion bounds.

In round-3 form: $\kappa$ is the same, and $\ell(v)=\kappa+v^2/4$ is no larger on $X^V$ than the manuscript's bound on $X$, so the conclusion "no Seiberg–Witten class at Feehan–Leness level $\ge0$ once $3n>7m$" survives.

*Proof.*
- (i): $c_e|_{N_V}=-e_j\mathrm{PD}(F_r)+e_{j+1}\mathrm{PD}(F'_l)$ pairs with $F_r,F'_l$ to $2e_j$ and $-2e_{j+1}$.
- (ii): orthogonal projection and negative definiteness.
- (iii): in the coordinates of Lemma 3.1, $v$ has $0$, $2$ or $4$ odd coordinates, so $v^2\equiv-\#\{\text{odd}\}\pmod4$.

**Prop. 3.4 (reducible flat connections on $V$).** Let $\rho$ be any reducible flat connection on $V$, possibly with stabilizer $U(1)$ (these exist when $H_1(V)$ has elements of order greater than 2). Then the weighted ASD index of $\rho$ equals that of the trivial connection, namely $-3$. Non-flat reducibles have index $8\kappa-3$, with $\kappa=-v^2/4\ge0$.

*Proof.*
1. The restriction of $\rho$ to $Y_{\pm2}$ is reducible. Its holonomy factors through $H_1(Y_{\pm2})=\mathbb Z/2$, so it is central, and $\operatorname{ad}\rho|_{\partial V}$ is the trivial flat bundle.
2. In the APS formula, the boundary terms depend only on $\operatorname{ad}\rho|_{\partial V}$. The interior integrand is $\operatorname{rank}(\operatorname{ad})$ times the Euler and signature densities, plus a $p_1$-density that vanishes for flat connections. Both are the same as for $\theta_V$.
3. Hence $\operatorname{ind}\rho=\operatorname{ind}\theta_V=-3(1-b_1+b^+)=-3$.
4. Exclusion of limiting configurations through $\rho$ uses only additivity of the index and regularity of the irreducible pieces. The stabilizer of $\rho$ does not enter.

∎ **[V]** for the index identity. **[P]** that nothing else uses the stabilizer; B4's extra hypothesis is not needed.

**Prop. 3.5 (gluing count with $V$-pieces).** Stretch all external $Y$-seams, as in `stack:composition`. Each copy of $V$ is now a separate factor with two seams. Use the local lower bounds on index excess:
- caps $\ge0$;
- single intervals $\ge0$, also with central outer limits (`stack:local-excess`);
- macros $\ge-2a$;
- irreducible $V$-pieces $\ge0$;
- reducible $V$-pieces $\ge-3$, which forces both of their seams to carry central states;
- each seam with a central state contributes $+3$.

Single intervals separate macros, so each $V$ is adjacent to at most one macro. Let $S$ be the number of central seams and $R_V$ the number of reducible $V$-pieces. Then
$$\text{total excess}\ \ge\ 3S-2A-3R_V\ \ge\ S-R_V\ \ge\ \tfrac12S .$$
The total excess is $0$, so $S=0$, and the count equals the composition as in the manuscript.

*Proof.*
- Seams adjacent to reducible $V$'s are distinct and number $2R_V$.
- Macro central limits at those seams number at most $R_V$; at the other seams, at most $S-2R_V$.

`checks.py` §5 checks this by dynamic programming over periodic patterns of up to 50 repetitions: the minimum excess with $S\ge1$ is $1$. The same check shows the bound fails ($-1$) when two macros meet through a single $V$. ∎ **[V]**

**Remark 3.6 (Floer reading) [V arithmetic].** On $V$ with $c=0$, B1 Prop. 1.1 gives $n_D(A)=\sigma_{t'}(\alpha')-\sigma_t(\alpha)$ for every instanton $A$. So:
- the CS level is $-\eta_V$ and the degree is $0$;
- the spin level is $L_\sigma=-\Theta_V=0$.

$V$ therefore has the same spin shift as $\varphi$ (zero), and the period $(VB)^3HB$ has shift $-\tfrac18$, as with $\varphi$. Only the telescoped total enters the closed argument, so no per-piece monotonicity on $V$ is required.

**Remark 3.7 (non-trivial $w_2(V)$) [P].** Suppose $w=w_2(V)\ne0$ with $w|_{\partial V}=0$.
- Then $\Lambda_0|_{N_V}\equiv w$ plus a characteristic class. Its maximum has $\Theta_{\rm cell}=k/4$, where $k$ is the number of coordinates on which $w$ is odd. This is formally favourable: spin shift $-k/4$ per $V$.
- Natural candidates show there is no free gain. For $V\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^2$ with $w=E$:
  - the odd blow-up series has no constant term†, so the map vanishes without an $e$-insertion;
  - an $e$-insertion of degree 2 raises $8\kappa$ by 2, which cancels the gain of $\tfrac14$ exactly.
- Theorem A is therefore stated with $w=0$. The odd $c$ in DLME's $g_1$ (with $\Theta_{\rm cell}=\tfrac14$ and no insertion) is the $\pm1$ instance of this favourable mechanism.

### 4.3 $\pm1$: the method is DLME's argument in closed form

**Setting.** Take the composite of $n$ copies of $Vg_1$, with $g_1$ integral and $V\colon Y_{-1}\to Y_1$ a negative-definite mod-2 equivalence. Take caps from $T'=f_-V'f_+$, with $f_+\colon Y_0\to Y_1$ and $f_-\colon Y_{-1}\to Y_0$. Each interval adds one parameter and no insertion, so
$$8\kappa=z+3+3b_0-n.$$

**Energy vanishing.** For $n>z+3+3b_0$ the zero-dimensional count needs $\kappa<0$. The moduli space is then empty for every metric in the family (energy $\ge-\varepsilon$ under small perturbation), so $\Omega=0$. The 2-adic repetition gives $\Omega\equiv\pm q\pmod{2^N}$, a contradiction.

**Prop. 4.1 [P].** Assume an integral lift of $g_1$ (DLME's formula $\hat c$-count minus $\check c$-count is already a difference), or a cap pairing that is odd. Then, for nontrivial $K$, there is no pair of negative-definite mod-2 equivalences $V\colon Y_{-1}\to Y_1$ and $V'\colon Y_1\to Y_{-1}$ with $V'$ satisfying the $\pi_1$ condition.

The proof is the closed-form $\ell$-argument (B3 Lemma C), with hypothesis (b) supplied by energy rather than by the Dirac index. **[V arithmetic; P for the integral $g_1$]**

**Comparison with DLME.**

| | DLME (Floer form) | Closed form |
|---|---|---|
| shift per interval | $L-D/8=-\tfrac18-\eta(K)$ | $8\kappa$ drops by 1 |
| strictness | from $\eta>0$ | from repetition; $\eta$ is not needed |
| nonvanishing | $\ell<\infty\Leftarrow I\ne0$ | caps with $q\ne0$; this needs $V'$ |
| conclusion | one-sided | two-sided |
| coefficients | $\mathbb F_2$ suffice | needs $\mathbb Z$ (or $q$ odd) |

**What this says about correctness.**
- **(a) [P]** It validates the ordinary layer and the logic of capping plus repetition: an argument of this shape proves a known theorem.
- **(b) [V]** It exercises *nothing* in the $\mathrm{PU}(2)$ layer. At $\pm1$ the vanishing is the trivial "no instantons of negative energy". At $\pm2$, $\kappa$ grows by $\tfrac18$ per interval and $\tfrac38$ per bridge, so energy can never vanish, as DLME's closing remark predicts. The vanishing must come from $n_D\ge1$, a Witten-conjecture-type statement. The two mechanisms differ in kind, so recovering DLME is not evidence for the new part.
- **(c) [V arithmetic]** The spin bookkeeping is consistent at $\pm1$:
  - $\Theta_{\rm cell}=\tfrac14$, so $n_D$ grows by $\tfrac38$ per interval;
  - the composite without $\varphi$, $(Hg_1)^n$, has $\kappa$ growing by $\tfrac14$ (no energy contradiction, correctly) and $n_D$ growing by $1$ per period (spin shifts $-\tfrac38-\tfrac58$).

  So the $\mathrm{PU}(2)$ vanishing must fail for bridges at spacing one at $\pm1$ as well. The manuscript's only internal test of its $\mathrm{PU}(2)$ layer is negative: the dimension count must fail without $\varphi$. It does fail formally: the run lemmas fail at spacing $\le2$ (fixed type) and at spacing $3$ (mixed type) (B4 §4).
- **(d) [S] A sharper test.** For any nontrivial $K$, the $SO(3)$-monopole relation on the 4-manifold capping $(HB)^M$ must have *nonzero* abelian and Seiberg–Witten contributions congruent to $2^{n_D-1}q$. Locating them (e.g. the explicit configurations that survive at spacing 1–2) in one example would test the exclusion lemmas.

### 4.4 $\pm1/n$

**Prop. 4.2 (DLME, one-sided) [V from DLME].** Let $K$ be nontrivial and $n\ge1$. There is no cobordism $V\colon S^3_{-1/n}(K)\to S^3_{1/n}(K)$ with $b_1=b^+=0$ and a surface $c\subset V$ such that $(V,c)_*$ is injective on $I(\,\cdot\,;\mathbb F_2)$.

*Proof.* Prop. 3.3 gives $L-D/8=-\eta(V,c)\le0$, so `kappa-ineq`(b) gives $\ell(Y_{1/n})\le\ell(Y_{-1/n})$. DLME's main inequality is $\ell(Y_{-1/n})<\ell(Y_{1/n})-\tfrac18$, a contradiction. ∎

The opposite direction $Y_{1/n}\to Y_{-1/n}$ is not obstructed, and DLME's ribbon theorem is the special case of a homology cobordism.

The closed form does not apply directly for $n\ge2$:
- $\Delta(1/n,-1/n)=2n$, so no distance-two triangle relates the two surgeries;
- DLME's chain consists of injections, not isomorphisms, so the closed-form $\ell$-argument (B3 Lemma C) has no endomorphism to iterate.

Conjecture C is the $\pm2$ statement of exactly this form.

## 5. Final statements and corollaries

**Main Theorem.** Let $K\subset S^3$ be a nontrivial knot, of any genus. Then $S^3_2(K)$ and $S^3_{-2}(K)$ cannot be joined by negative-definite cobordisms in both directions that induce isomorphisms on Floer's instanton homology mod 2. Precisely, there are no $V\colon S^3_{-2}(K)\to S^3_2(K)$ and $V'\colon S^3_2(K)\to S^3_{-2}(K)$ such that:
- $b_1=b^+=0$ for both;
- the trivial $SO(3)$-bundle maps are isomorphisms on $I_*(\,\cdot\,;\mathbb F_2)$;
- $\pi_1(V')$ is normally generated by the image of one end.

**[S]**: conditional on §§6–10. The reduction is [V]/[P] as in §2.

**Cor. 5.1 (purely cosmetic surgery conjecture).** Combine $V=$ the mapping cylinder of $\varphi$, $V'=$ that of $\varphi^{-1}$, with Ni–Wu†, Hanselman† and DLME Thm. 1.2. Then no nontrivial knot has distinct purely cosmetic slopes. **[S]**, conditional exactly as the manuscript is.

**Cor. 5.2 (ribbon cobordisms).** For nontrivial $K$, $S^3_{\pm2}(K)$ are not related, in either direction, by a ribbon homology cobordism.
- **Proof.** Let $X$ be ribbon from one to the other, and $\bar X$ its reverse. Both have $b_2=0$ and $b_1=0$. Relative to its outgoing end, $X$ has only 2- and 3-handles, so $\pi_1(\text{outgoing})\to\pi_1(X)$ is onto; this gives the $\pi_1$ condition for whichever of $X,\bar X$ plays $V'$. Daemi–Lidman–Vela-Vick–Wong† give $X_*$ injective and $\bar X_*$ surjective. Equal ranks then give isomorphisms. ∎
- **Status.** **[S]** (Main Theorem), plus **[P†]** that the injectivity theorem extends to rational homology spheres with central reducibles and to rational homology cobordisms. This is the DLME phrasing ("cannot be related by a ribbon homology cobordism").

**Cor. 5.3 (cyclic chains).** If $K_1,\dots,K_r$ are knots with $S^3_{-2}(K_i)\cong S^3_2(K_{i+1})$ (orientation-preserving, indices mod $r$), then all $K_i$ are trivial. This is the $\pm2$ analogue of DLME's cyclic theorem.
- **Caps.** Use the return $\varphi_1^{-1}H_{K_2}\varphi_2^{-1}\cdots H_{K_r}\varphi_r^{-1}\colon Y_2(K_1)\to Y_{-2}(K_1)$. It is simply connected. Its $b^+=r-1$ comes only through admissible $Y_0$'s, so the central contacts still cost 3.
- **Composite cobordism.** Use the period $B_{K_1}\varphi_1\cdots B_{K_r}\varphi_r$ with bridges $H_{K_i}$ at spacing 4.
- **Cells.** They are local: $\langle-1\rangle^2$, and $\mathbb{CP}^2\#5\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^2$ for every knot.
- **Nontriviality.** The rank of $I$ is constant along the chain, so if one $K_i$ is nontrivial then all are, and every $I\ne0$.
- **Status.** **[S]**; the extension of the proof is **[P]**.

**Cor. 5.4 (amphichiral knots).** For nontrivial amphichiral $K$, $S^3_2(K)$ admits no orientation-reversing self-diffeomorphism. More strongly, there is no pair $W,W'$ of negative-definite mod-2 equivalences $-Y\to Y$ with $Y=S^3_2(K)$ and $W'$ satisfying the $\pi_1$ condition. **[S]**. This is a special case of the Main Theorem, since $S^3_{-2}(K)=-S^3_2(K)$.

**Cor. 5.5 (Chern–Simons statements).** These are Theorem B(i)–(ii): no pair with $\eta(V)+\eta(V')>0$, and no $V$ with $\eta(V)\ge\tfrac18$. Also, a cosmetic $\varphi$ forces $\eta(W',c)\le\frac18$ for a lift of $B$. **[P]**, independent of the $\mathrm{PU}(2)$ layer. This is the most reliable new statement here.

**Remark 5.6 (exotic structures) [S].** This is the $\pm2$ analogue of the construction DLME quote from [LLP, Prop. 1.15]. Glue the ends of $W_H$ by $\varphi$. The resulting $Z_\varphi$ has:
- $\pi_1=\mathbb Z$, $\chi=4$, $b_2=4$, $\sigma=-2$, $b^+=1$ **[V]** (from $W_H$: four 2-handles, and $H_2(W_H;\mathbb Q)=\{\textstyle\sum b_i\text{ even}\}$ with form $(\sum b)^2/2-\sum b^2$);
- an odd form, since $\sigma\not\equiv0\pmod 8$.

So $Z_\varphi$ has the homology and intersection form of $S^1\times S^3\#\mathbb{CP}^2\#3\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^2$; plausibly it is homeomorphic to it (Freedman–Quinn for $\pi_1=\mathbb Z$†). The Main Theorem says no exotic structure arises this way. Whether $Z_\varphi$ would be exotic is not known; I have not checked LLP's argument.

## 6. Weak points

1. **Degree bookkeeping [P].** Round 2's degrees give $D_B\in\{-1,7\}$ and $D_H=-3$, so $\deg(HB)\equiv4\pmod 8$. Then "$HB\simeq\mathrm{id}$ mod 2" can hold only up to the degree-4 action of the flat $\mathbb Z/2$-bundle (Floer's $H^1(Y;\mathbb Z/2)$-action), or one of these degrees is off. This does not affect the 2-adic argument, which needs only an automorphism mod 2. It also does not affect the per-map no-go, which needs only injectivity.
2. **Rereading.** I did not reread §§6–7 and §§9–10 line by line for an *equality* use of the negative-cell form. The §8 statements explicitly discard negative squares.
3. **Transversality.** Transversality of split components and Dirac operators on pieces containing $V$ is assumed to follow from the same generic perturbations as on $N_\varphi$. Nothing in §1 locates geometry on $\varphi$-cells.
4. **Citations.** Donaldson's theorem for non-simply-connected manifolds, the injectivity theorem for ribbon cobordisms between rational homology spheres, the odd blow-up series, and the $\pi_1=\mathbb Z$ classification are cited from memory†.
5. **Theorem B's assumptions.** Theorem B assumes DLME's $IP$-modules exist for $Y_{\pm2}$ and that $B$ is filtered with level $-\eta_B$ as a family map with insertion. Both are standard in form but not written down.
