\section{Panel: four angles, no triangle meets the requirements}

The requirements were (1) an injective connecting map, (2) of topological level exactly
$-\frac1{16}$, supporting flat connections in the mixed case, and (3) exact at
$T(2,-3)$ and $4_1$.

orbifold, pillowcase (found independently) & $F:\ (Y_2,P_\varepsilon)\to(Y_{-2},P_{1-\varepsilon})\to I(Y_0)\oplus I(Y_0,w)$. \st{Pillowcase classification (proved in the Fukaya model).} Every triangle with vertices
$L_2^\varepsilon$ and $L_{-2}^{1-\varepsilon}$ is the cone of a map spanned by the two
mixed points.
\begin{itemize}
\item The backward cones have middle term $V_\alpha\oplus E_0\oplus E_{1/2}$.
  \begin{itemize}
  \item $\alpha=\frac14$ gives the known triangles (I) and (II).
  \item $\alpha\ne\frac14$ gives Triangle($\alpha$). Its cap areas $4(\alpha-\frac18)^2$ and $4(\alpha-\frac38)^2$ differ, so the theory is non-monotone.
  \item $\alpha\to0$ gives the Farey route, with middle term $0$ but level $\ge\frac18$.
  \end{itemize}
\item The forward cone is $F$.
\end{itemize}
Nothing else exists.

\section{The sharpness obstruction}

\begin{prop}[N2]
Let $f\colon A_1\to B_0$ be defined uniformly in $K$ by counting, possibly broken, instantons
on cobordisms or families that contain $E_K\times[0,1]$ compatibly with both ends. This covers
surgery, singular, orbifold and lens-capped cobordisms, families, composites, Toda brackets and
pillowcase polygons. If $f$ is injective at $K=4_1$, then $L_{\rm top}(f)>-\frac1{16}$.
\end{prop}

\noindent\emph{Proof.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item For $4_1$ we have $\sigma=0$, so the correction of \S3 does not enter. Both complexes are perfect: $A_1$ has its bottom class at bd, level $\frac15$, and $B_0=\{z\}$ at level $\frac{11}{80}=\frac15-\frac1{16}$.
\item An injective map of level $-\frac1{16}$ must send $x_{\rm bd}$ to $z$ through an instanton of energy $0$, i.e.\ a flat connection.
\item A flat connection restricts to a single representation of $\pi_1(E_K\times I)$. But bd and $z$ are different representations: bd is binary dihedral with $a=\frac14$, and $z$ is not. $\square$
\end{enumerate}

\st{Consequences.}
\begin{itemize}
\item In this toolkit, no single injective map proves the sharp bound $m_2\ge\frac1{16}$.
\item If levels lie in $\frac1{16}\mathbb Z$, as every row of the arithmetic table does, the best possible outcome is $m_2>0$, coming from a level-$0$ map plus positivity of energy.
\item A flat connection realising equality must change the $E_K$-representation. It therefore lives on a cobordism that does \emph{not} contain $E_K\times I$ compatibly.
\item It also cannot pass through a vertex carrying only reducibles, if that vertex is reached by 2- and 3-handles (topology, O3). The holonomy image of bd is dihedral ($D_3$ for $T(2,-3)$, $D_5$ for $4_1$) and has no element of order $4$, so a $\gamma_1$ end never occurs.
\end{itemize}
In the data the equality is carried by $b'\circ a'$: $a'$ is flat at bd, and $b'$ has energy
exactly $\frac1{16}$, the area of the mixed triangle.

\st{Escapes left.}
\begin{itemize}
\item A cobordism $Y_2\to Y_{-2}$ not containing $E_K\times I$. Its flat part must change representation, e.g.\ by adding $1$-handles.
\item A non-topological cancellation.
\item Settling for a strict bound $m_2>0$. This needs a non-null mixed map of topological level $0$ that is nonzero on the bottom class. The known ones, $W'_{\rm odd}$ and $b\circ a$, are null.
\end{itemize}

\section{The corrected complex for $(Y_{\pm2},w)$}

\st{Reducible index (\texttt{reducible-index.md}).}
\begin{itemize}
\item On $Y_1\to(Y_2,w)$, the twisted reducible $A_k=\mathbb R\oplus L$ with $c_1(L)=(2k+1)e$ has $e^2=-\frac12$ and energy $(2k+1)^2/8$.
\item Its $L$-part has complex index $\frac{(2k+1)^2-1}2+\frac{\rho_\chi(Y_2)}2$, where $\rho_\chi(Y_{\pm2})=\sigma(K)$ in the APS convention (Casson--Gordon).
\item So the minimal reducible ($\kappa=\frac18$) is obstructed with cokernel $\mathbb C^{|\sigma|/2}$ on one of the two edges at $(Y_{\pm2},w)$.
\item This gives $|\sigma|/2$ suspensions, and none when $\sigma=0$, in particular when $\Delta_K=1$.
\end{itemize}

\st{Your rule, applied.} I added $|\sigma|/2$ generators with Chern--Simons equal to that of
$\zeta$. I read the degrees as ${\rm ind}(\zeta\to\cdot)$. Read as ${\rm ind}(\cdot\to\zeta)$
instead, the excess cases are inconsistent: the new and old generators have the same parity,
so the rank cannot come out right. In the deficit cases the new generators survive. In the
excess cases each one cancels an old generator, constrained by the $\mathbb Z/8$ grading and
by $E>0$. Data: the $105$ torus knots with $pq\le120$, in both chiralities.

\noindent For positive knots the surviving new generators of $(Y_2,w)$ sit at levels
$\frac{1+|\sigma|}8,\frac{|\sigma|-1}8,\dots$, ending at \emph{exactly} $\frac38$. So
$\lambda_1(Y_2)=\frac38<\lambda_0(Y_{-2})$.

\emph{Caveat.} This depends on two normalisations. An offset of $2$ in degree moves the
bottom generator to $\frac58$. Taking Chern--Simons to be $0$ in the absolute normalisation,
rather than at $\zeta$, moves it to $\frac14$. The mirror-knot column depends on the same choices; in this reading no mirror-knot drop decreases.

\section{Calibration for general knots}

\begin{itemize}
\item The sharp, non-strict $\frac1{16}$ cannot come from any map built on $E_K\times I$ (N2). Equality at $4_1$ is witnessed by positive energy on a triangle face.
\item What such maps can give is a strict bound from a level-$0$ map. Even that needs a non-null mixed map that is injective on the bottom class, and none is known.
\item The $-\frac1{16}$ maps that do exist are $F$'s side map, $\mathcal E_{\gamma_1}$ and the $s=\frac12$ singular maps of $\lambda'$. All are null, go to or from a singular theory, or are not flat at bd.
\end{itemize}
